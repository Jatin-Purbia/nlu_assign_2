  Architecture Comparison Discussion
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  VanillaRNN:
    The simplest model.  Suffers from vanishing gradients over long names,
    causing it to forget early characters.  Common failure: names that start
    well but deteriorate into repeated consonants at the end.  Good at learning
    common short name endings (e.g. "-a", "-sh", "-an") but poor at capturing
    the phonological structure of longer South Indian names.

  BidirectionalLSTM:
    The LSTM gating mechanism (forget / input / output gates) alleviates
    vanishing gradients significantly.  The bidirectional encoder provides
    richer gradient signal during training, so the model learns the co-occurrence
    of characters at different positions within a name.  Generated names tend to
    be more coherent and structurally similar to real Indian names.  However,
    the decoder is unidirectional at inference, so it cannot "look ahead".
    Common failure: occasionally generates plausible prefixes with incoherent
    suffixes (e.g. "Pra---vix" where the suffix drifts).

  AttentionRNN:
    The attention mechanism allows the decoder to selectively attend back to
    any position in the previously generated prefix.  This is particularly
    beneficial for Indian names that have repeated syllable patterns
    (e.g. "Venkatesh" benefits from attending back to "Ven" when generating
    "katesh").  At higher temperatures, this model produces the most diverse
    outputs.  Common failure: at very low temperatures (T=0.5), the model
    collapses to a small set of common name prefixes ("Raj", "San", "Man").
    At high temperatures (T=1.5), it can generate non-Indian-sounding names.
